# Direction and review record

## Separate creative work

The two 20-shot films were written and directed independently for this request.
They are not outputs of the old short comparison fixtures. Psychology has 832 narrated
words, a warm visual design, a female voice and a personal motivation story. What-if
has 905 narrated words, a cool visual design, a male voice and a fictional city outage
story. Their set-piece libraries do not overlap.

The first workflow uses a reviewed, source-informed script. The second uses a clearly
labelled fictional scenario with a narrow factual foundation for how networks differ
from power, local storage and radio. Neither script was represented as an unreviewed
model fact-check. These first films are authored review plans; future blank-plan runs
invoke the local writer against the new title and brief.

## Refinements made before production

- Replaced repeated generic prop arrangements with story-specific animated sets.
- Moved supporting actors away from illustrated objects to avoid occluding them.
- Added close writing and phone shots to vary scale and show hands using objects.
- Made the resting stance more upright and constrained the walking pelvis to reachable
  planted feet. Kept the continuous benchmark's post-stop stance physically reachable.
- Blended upper-body acting beats while preserving planted feet, then solved arm IK.
  QA measures the same directed poses as the renderer.
- Gave the reconnection sequence genuinely progressive links instead of displaying
  every connection as already repaired.
- Reduced the psychology ending's burden to a chosen reason rather than repeating
  the opening stack of imposed expectations.

## Inspection scope

Reviewed all forty directed storyboard frames and four-time-point sequences for
walking, running, writing, distraction, radio, reconnection and the closing phone call.
Rendered complete draft MP4s, inspected sampled rendered frames, and ran media decoding,
black/freeze detection, narration-presence, audio-headroom and motion-contact checks.
Final production checks and artifact links are recorded in the deliverables page.

The visual review is a frame and sequence inspection, not a claim of a complete human
watch-and-listen session. Technical passing checks do not award a professional artistic
score. The films use original procedural 2.5D art and a finite acting library; this is
an inspectable implementation, not a claim of unrestricted studio-level animation.
