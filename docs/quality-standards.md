# Quality standards and acceptance boundary

Technical QA gates: nonempty playable MP4, full decode, exact preset resolution and
FPS, H.264/yuv420p, stereo AAC 48 kHz, requested duration ±25%, contiguous scene timing,
measured audio present and fitting, conservative black/freeze detection, peak below
-0.1 dBFS, contact sliding below 2 design pixels/second, reach error below 2 pixels,
ground penetration below 0.1 pixels and leg length error below 0.1 pixels.

Those numeric gates do not establish good acting, meaningful staging, scene variety,
humor, scientific accuracy, lip-sync or emotional impact. The JSON report explicitly
keeps artistic readiness pending. No fabricated quality scores or professional label.
Segment presence checks do not prove every spoken word was pronounced; ASR/listening
qualification is still needed. Full playback remains essential.

Review artifacts contain twelve extracted frames, a six-second motion excerpt and
original media. Review the benchmark's start, contact, acceleration, deceleration,
turn and pickup. Review longer scenes, not just a contact sheet. Static pictures can
show proportions/composition but cannot prove that timing and movement feel natural.

Known limitations include a finite, symbolic action/prop library; simplified stairs,
falls and seated interactions; no general collision/physics; shared rig proportions;
limited head-turn projection; basic score/foley; approximate caption word timing;
no expressive TTS control; no autonomous fact retrieval or source entailment checks;
no audience study. Meaningful production upgrades require bespoke shot direction,
new clips/props and human creative review, not just higher FPS.

The history and survival comparisons are explicitly fictional. Science/security/scam
sources are logged in `source-verification.json`; those pages were retrieved and read
for relevant claims. Model-generated factual scripts still need review against their
source notes. Never interpret the presence of a URL as proof of accuracy.
